01 / Geocentric
A loop added to a circle

Ptolemy's Almagest (c. 150 CE): an eccentric deferent, an equant and an epicycle, with his values for Mars (deferent 60, eccentricity 6, epicycle 39;30).

02 / Heliocentric
A change of vantage point

Copernicus's De revolutionibus (1543): Earth's motion makes the loop. An off-centre deferent and a small epicyclet do the equant's job (Mars eccentricity 1460, epicyclet 500, in parts of 10,000).

03 / Tychonic
One sky, two frames

Tycho Brahe (1588): the Copernican geometry seen from a fixed Earth, so its sky is identical to model 02. Ptolemy's equant puts Mars within 1° of both over this span.

About this model. Three-dimensional reconstructions using each astronomer’s own Mars parameters. Longitudes: Ptolemy’s Almagest X, Copernicus’s De revolutionibus V, and Copernicus’s values again in Tycho’s Earth-fixed frame, with Tycho’s solar eccentricity of 0.0358. Latitudes: Ptolemy’s eccentric tilted 1° and his rocking epicycle (2;15 to the eccentric at the limits, parallel to the ecliptic at the nodes, varied here as a cosine), and Copernicus’s orbit tilt rocking from 0;10 to 1;50 with Earth’s position. The Tychonic model uses Copernicus’s latitudes. All three share mean periods of 365.25 and 686.98 days and Ptolemy’s apsidal lines, with Mars’s northern limit at its apogee. Omitted: the slow drift of the apsides and nodes, and Copernicus’s variable solar eccentricity. Kepler’s ellipses (1609), in planes through the true Sun, replaced all three.
